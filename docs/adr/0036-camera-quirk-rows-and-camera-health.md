# ADR-0036: Camera quirks as data rows, a guard against damaged frames, and the camera's health as an offer, never an automatic change

- **Status:** Accepted (2026-11-13). The quirk rows, the corrupt-frame guard and the colour-range relabel were implemented in daemon 0.45.6 (`crates/obc-pipeline`: `quirks`, `frame_guard`, `colour_range`; D-Bus revision 19 unchanged); camera health and the recommended settings were implemented in daemon 0.46.0, with D-Bus revision 20 (`crates/obc-pipeline`: `delivered_rate`, `usb_topology`; `crates/obc-ipc`: `health`; `crates/omarchy-broadcastd`: `health`, the controller; `crates/omarchy-broadcast`: `recommended`). Builds on [ADR-0009](0009-ingest-process-output.md) (the camera belongs to the ingest stage), [ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md) (the controls are the user's; they are written whenever the camera opens and never to a closed camera), [ADR-0023](0023-settings-schema.md) (the settings tree and the quirk rows that hide controls) and [ADR-0033](0033-system-memory-pipeline.md) (the range conversion in system memory, whose "remedy: a per-camera data row" this builds). Amends [ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md) in one respect: the power-line frequency is written at an open from the time zone when nothing is stored, the one value the daemon sets without being asked. Reference: [../dbus-api.md](../dbus-api.md#camera-health-revision-20), [../cli-json.md](../cli-json.md#recommended-settings-and-camera-health), [../architecture.md](../architecture.md#camera-health-and-recommended-settings), [../testing.md](../testing.md#camera-health-and-recommended-settings).

## Context
- UVC webcams of different makes behave differently in ways the standard interfaces do not describe. Cameras were found that send full-range pixel values under a limited-range label, cameras that accept a control write and change nothing, a camera whose manual white balance write fails and turns the picture yellow-green, cameras that deliver 19.5 or 20 fps for a mode they list at 30, and cameras whose pictures are grainy at ordinary light. None of it can be learnt from the device at run time without a measurement.
- The design must stay free of per-camera code ([../architecture.md](../architecture.md)): a new camera must be a change of data, and the model, the tree and the clients must not change.
- GStreamer 1.28's `v4l2src` passes on a buffer the driver dequeued with `V4L2_BUF_FLAG_ERROR`, marked `GST_BUFFER_FLAG_CORRUPTED` (`uvcvideo` delivers such frames by default, `nodrop=1`), drops a raw buffer only when it is shorter than the frame, and never checks the size of an MJPEG buffer. A torn or truncated JPEG therefore reached the decoder and the output.
- GStreamer 1.28.8's `videoconvert` and `videoconvertscale` do not convert a range-only difference ([ADR-0033](0033-system-memory-pipeline.md)), so a camera that mislabels its range cannot be corrected by trusting the converter on the system-memory path.
- A camera on a USB hub shares the hub's link with everything below it. A raw (uncompressed) stream of 640x480 YUYV at 30 fps moves 18.4 MB/s, close to what a High-Speed link carries in practice. A Microsoft LifeCam VX-800 streaming it on a hub that also carried a keyboard and a mouse reset both devices and lost 14 to 22 % of its frames; a Logitech C930e streaming MJPEG on the same hub, beside the same keyboard and mouse, lost none.
- The camera's controls are the user's ([ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md)): other software changes them, the daemon writes the stored ones at every open, and nothing the daemon decides by itself may overwrite what the user chose.
- The panel needs to say what is wrong with a camera and what could help, which the daemon alone can measure: the rate really delivered, the damaged frames, where the camera sits on the bus.

## Decision
**Behaviour of a camera model is a row of data keyed by USB id. The ingest drops damaged frames and says how many. A raw camera's colour range is relabelled so the existing converters do the right thing. The daemon reports the camera's health, and offers settings measured for its model, but applies them only when asked; the one automatic write is the power-line frequency of the time zone.**

### 1. Quirks are rows keyed by USB vendor and product id
`obc_pipeline::quirks` holds four kinds of row, looked up by the camera's USB id (found in sysfs by the node's device number, as for the Extension Unit rows of [ADR-0023](0023-settings-schema.md)):
- **Range rows** (`RANGE_QUIRKS`): the model and the raw formats it mislabels. MJPEG is never listed, because decoded JPEG is full range and labelled so.
- **Hidden-control rows** (`CONTROL_QUIRKS`): standard controls the settings model leaves out, with why (`NoEffect`: the camera accepts the write and nothing changes; `Broken`: the write fails or breaks the picture), and `reset_at_open`: hidden controls written back to their default at every open, so that a camera another program left in a broken state recovers. A row may list only controls whose default write is known to be safe, and a test checks that each is also hidden.
- **Camera profiles** (`CAMERA_PROFILES`): the control values offered as the camera's *recommended settings* (every other control at its default; an empty list means the defaults are best) and `noisy`, whether its picture is noisy enough to suggest noise removal. A profile never names a hidden control and never the power-line frequency.
- **Extension Unit rows** ([ADR-0023](0023-settings-schema.md)), unchanged.

A row records a measurement on that model. Today: the Logitech MX Brio, the Generalplus and the VX-800 have range rows; the Generalplus (brightness, contrast, hue) and the VX-800 (the white balance switch and temperature) have hidden-control rows; the laptop FHD camera (sharpness 5, hue 0, noisy), the C930e (defaults, not noisy), the Generalplus (defaults, noisy) and the VX-800 (defaults, noisy) have profiles.

**The run-time range check only suggests.** A raw camera labelled limited that has no row is sampled on its first frames (the luma of six frames, every 8th pixel in both directions), and when at least 1 % of the samples lie below 14 or above 237 one log line suggests adding a row. Nothing changes while the camera streams.

### 2. The corrupt-frame guard
A buffer probe on the camera source's `src` pad (`obc_pipeline::frame_guard`), installed when the head is built and so before the statistics probes attach, drops:
- any buffer flagged `GST_BUFFER_FLAG_CORRUPTED`, in any format, and
- in MJPEG mode, a buffer that does not start with the start-of-image marker (`FF D8`) or has no end-of-image marker (`FF D9`) within its last 1 KiB (a camera may pad a frame after the marker).

Raw frames are never inspected beyond the flag: any pattern of pixel values is a frame. The order of the probes matters: GStreamer stops at the first probe that drops a buffer, so the statistics, which keep every frame they see until its output tick, never record a dropped frame. A dropped frame is simply missing and the output clock repeats the last good one, as for any frame the camera did not deliver.

**Two graces of 15 frames keep a picture on screen.** A camera that ends its JPEGs without the end marker would have every frame dropped by the second check; a camera or USB path that flags every payload would have every frame dropped by the first. When the first 15 MJPEG frames of a stream all lack the end marker, or the first 15 frames dropped for the flag come before any frame has passed, that check is switched off for the rest of the stream and a line says so once (a frame that is flagged is then judged like any other, and an MJPEG one must still be a whole JPEG). A stream that has passed one frame keeps both checks: its errors are then real and transient. A camera that needs both graces shows the placeholder for at most 30 frames, one second at 30 fps.

The drops are **counted** per cause, **logged** (the first at once, then a summary at most every 10 s, and the pending counts at stop) and **reported**: the total is `corrupt_frames` of `CameraHealth`, and a share of 5 % or more over 10 s draws a warning (section 4).

### 3. The colour range of a raw camera is relabelled, by memory
For a camera with a range row the ingest does not touch the pixel data except in the second case:
- **CUDA memory:** the frames are relabelled full range right after the camera's caps filter, the matrix, transfer function and primaries of the label kept. `cudaconvertscale` then converts range together with the scale, as for any full-range source.
- **System memory, limited contract:** GStreamer 1.28.8's converter ignores a range-only difference, so the ingest applies the exact levels with `videobalance` (the MJPEG path's constants: contrast 219/255, brightness 16·219/255², saturation 224/255) and relabels the frames limited range, again keeping matrix, transfer and primaries, so that a later converter that does check the range, as a newer GStreamer will, does not compress them a second time.
- **System memory, full contract:** the frames are relabelled full range, which they already are.

### 4. Camera health and recommended settings are an offer
D-Bus revision 20 adds the property `CameraHealth` (`a{sv}`) and the method `ApplyRecommendedSettings(s camera)`; `Stats` is unchanged (`dddd`).

- **The delivered rate is measured from good frames by their kernel timestamps.** The guard records the PTS of each frame it passes (`v4l2src` stamps a buffer from the kernel's buffer timestamp, so the spacing is the camera's own, not the streaming thread's). The rate is the number of frames less one over the span of a sliding window of about 5 s, the first 15 good frames of a stream not counted (a camera's first frames come irregularly while its exposure settles) and a rate reported once the window spans 3 s. No rate is reported once no good frame has come for 1 s. The controller keeps the last measurement per camera id and capture mode for the daemon's life, so it shows while the camera is idle, and publishes `-1` until measured. Measuring good frames only means that damaged frames lower the rate, as they lower what the output receives.
- **The label of the capture mode in use shows it** (`640x480 · 19.5 of 30 fps`) once the camera is measured below 95 % of the mode's rate. A label change is a structural change of the settings tree (clients read it again), so it has a hysteresis: on below 0.95, off above 0.97, the rate shown moves only when the delivered rate is 1.5 fps or more away from it, and at most every 30 s. A VX-800 swinging between 17 and 23 fps would otherwise move it at every poll.
- **Warnings are sentences for people.** *A shared hub:* the camera is below a hub other than the root hub that also carries HID devices (a device with a HID interface and no video interface, so a camera's own buttons do not count), **and** its mode is a raw one needing at least 12 MB/s. The rule is keyed on bandwidth, not on mere sharing: a compressed stream beside a keyboard and a mouse was measured clean, and an uncompressed one corrupted frames and reset the HID devices (Context). MJPEG, with no fixed rate, never draws it. *Damaged frames:* at least 5 % of the frames dropped over the last 10 s, whatever the port, on at 5 % and off below 3 %, with the hub advice added when the camera shares a hub and the first warning does not already say it.
- **Suggestions are hints, not changes.** Noise removal for a camera whose profile says it is noisy; enhancement for a camera whose largest mode is below 720 lines. A suggestion is omitted when the setting is on, and when the stage cannot run here (the setting is greyed); it is never the HDR look. The client writes the setting when the user asks.
- **`has_recommendation` and `recommended`.** `recommended` lists the changes `ApplyRecommendedSettings` would make, only where they differ from now, as the settings tree displays them.
- **`ApplyRecommendedSettings` is an explicit action** ([ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md): the controls are the user's). It resets the camera's device settings to their defaults (not the capture mode, not the effects), then writes the profile's values and the power-line frequency of the time zone, through the path of `SetSetting`, and stores them as the user's. The daemon never calls it; the CLI runs it on `recommended apply` and the panel on a click.
- **The power-line frequency is the one automatic write.** At an open, when nothing is stored for `power_line_frequency`, the daemon writes the 50 or 60 Hz of the time zone (`TZ`, else the `/etc/localtime` link, read once), if the camera offers it and the control can be written now. The value is not stored: it is no choice of the user's, and a stored value wins. Where a country has both grids (Japan), where the sources disagree, or where the zone is unknown, nothing is written. The frequency belongs to the region and not to the camera, so no row carries it.
- **Publication is throttled.** A change that appears or goes (the camera, a warning, a suggestion, the recommended changes) is announced at once; a change only the counters make is announced at most every 2 s and only when a published value changed (the rate is rounded to 0.1). The measurements are taken on the controller thread at the existing statistics tick: no thread, timer or descriptor is added.

### 5. One stream at a time
A camera switch stops the old ingest, with its camera closed, before it starts the next, so two ingests are never open at once (`a_switch_never_has_two_ingests_open`). This is the behaviour of the session ([ADR-0010](0010-phase-locked-output-clock.md): the output holds the last frame across the gap) made an explicit invariant, because the health rules rest on it: two streams, or a raw stream beside a keyboard and a mouse, share one USB 2.0 link, and damaged frames or stalled HID devices would be the daemon's own doing.

## Consequences
- A new camera's quirk is a row of data. The ingest, the settings model, the tree and the clients need no change, and a test checks that a model has no row twice and that no row hides a control a profile sets.
- A damaged frame never reaches the output; for a camera that sends many, the output repeats the last good frame, so the picture stutters instead of tearing. The count and the warning make the cause visible.
- A camera that is wrong in a way no row covers is carried as the camera labels it: a mislabelled range is corrected only for a listed model, and the run-time check only suggests a row.
- The recommended settings are measured for a handful of models today; a model with no row has `has_recommendation` false and no recommendation. The suggestions of noise removal and enhancement need a profile or a low-resolution camera, and the stage must be able to run.
- D-Bus revision 20 is additive. A client built for revision 19 keeps working: it ignores the property and the method, and shows the longer capture-mode label as a label. The CLI's JSON stays at `v` 1 (`camera_health` is a new field and a new line type).
- The delivered rate costs the guard a timestamp per good frame under a short lock and the controller one read per statistics tick.
- Tests: [../testing.md](../testing.md#camera-health-and-recommended-settings).

### Known limitations
- **The shared-hub rule reads sysfs.** A camera behind a hub that sysfs does not describe (a virtual machine, a USB-over-network device) has no placement and draws no hub warning; a damaged-frames warning still shows.
- **A rate is only as steady as the camera.** A camera whose frame interval swings (a long exposure in dim light) shows the average of the last 5 s, and its label moves by steps of 1.5 fps at most every 30 s.
- **Time zones are a proxy for the mains frequency.** A zone's name does not tell a region within a country with both grids; those, and zones the tables do not list, get nothing.
- **The recommended values are measurements of a camera model at ordinary light.** Another unit of the model, or another light, may prefer other values; the user can change them, and apply nothing.

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| **A branch in the code for each bad camera** | The design stays model-independent ([../architecture.md](../architecture.md)); a branch is a change of code, a row is a change of data, and a table is checked by tests (no model twice, a reset-at-open control is also hidden) |
| **Correcting a mislabelled range at run time, without a row** | A scene without highlights or shadows shows nothing either way, and a correctly labelled camera overshoots 16 to 235 by a level or two, so a threshold on the first frames would sometimes relabel a camera that is right. The check only logs a suggested row; a measured row is certain |
| **Trusting the converter for the range on the system-memory path** | GStreamer 1.28.8 does not convert a range-only difference ([ADR-0033](0033-system-memory-pipeline.md)); a test of the levels fails |
| **Converting the range through RGB** | Exact, but +2.1 ms per 1080p frame against +0.55 ms for `videobalance` |
| **Passing damaged frames and letting the decoder cope** | `v4l2src` does not check an MJPEG buffer, and a decoder that tolerates a torn JPEG shows a torn picture; the last good frame is better |
| **Dropping every buffer that fails a check for the whole stream** | A camera that flags every frame, or never sends the end marker, would show only the placeholder. The 15-frame graces switch the check off for such a stream |
| **Applying a camera's profile at every open or at first sight** | It would overwrite what the user chose and what other software set, against [ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md), for a measurement of one unit at ordinary light. The profile is an offer with the list of changes, taken on a click |
| **Warning for every camera that shares a hub** | A compressed stream beside a keyboard and a mouse was measured clean; the warning would be wrong for every compressed stream and teach the user to ignore it. The rule needs a raw mode of at least 12 MB/s |
| **Measuring the rate from the writer or by the wall clock of the streaming thread** | The writer's rate (`Stats.fps`) is the output's, repeated frames included; the streaming thread's clock adds the scheduler's jitter. The kernel's buffer timestamps are the camera's own |
| **Counting damaged frames in the rate, or publishing the camera's rate as it is measured** | A rate that includes the dropped frames would hide the loss; one published at every change would flood the bus and move the structure of the tree at every tick |
| **Putting health in `Stats`** | It would change the signal's signature (`dddd`) for every client, for values that change rarely and are read once |
| **A thread or timer for the health** | The statistics tick already runs on the controller thread; the measurements are one read of the ingest |
| **A profile row for the power-line frequency** | The frequency is the region's, not the camera's: the same model needs 50 Hz in one country and 60 Hz in another |
| **Storing the time zone's frequency as the user's value** | It would pin a value the user never chose, and keep it when the user moves to another region. Not stored, it follows the zone, and a value the user stores wins |
| **Asking the user for the power-line frequency** | A panel cannot know it for them, and a wrong value shows as flicker under artificial light; the zone is a good default that costs nothing |
| **Two ingests open during a switch, for a seamless handover** | Two streams on one USB 2.0 link, or beside a keyboard and a mouse, would cause the damage the health rules warn about; the output already holds the last frame across the gap |

## What would change this decision
- A GStreamer whose `v4l2src` drops flagged buffers and checks the size of an MJPEG buffer: the guard's two checks would become redundant, though they keep the output correct meanwhile. A test of the guard says when.
- A GStreamer whose converter checks the range: the `videobalance` step would become redundant on the system-memory path; the relabel keeps it correct meanwhile.
- A way to recognise a mislabelled range reliably at run time: the check could correct instead of suggest.
- Evidence that users want a recommended profile applied without asking for some models: that would be a decision of its own, against [ADR-0020](0020-camera-image-controls.md), for a model and a setting at a time.
- A panel that needs the delivered rate more often than every 2 s: a signal beside `Stats`, not a change to it.
