# ADR-0010: Phase-locked output clock, held frame at a switch, bounded output queue

- **Status:** Accepted. Amends [ADR-0009](0009-ingest-process-output.md) (output clock timing, switch behaviour, output queue size).

## Context
- The output clock of [ADR-0009](0009-ingest-process-output.md) emitted tick `n` at `n x period` on a fixed grid. A camera frame waits in the latest-frame slot until the next tick, anywhere from zero to one period (33.3 ms at 30 fps). A camera at the contract rate or a multiple of it keeps a nearly constant phase to that grid, so the wait is a nearly constant offset set by chance when the stream starts. Measured against the single pipeline it replaced, the median added latency rose from 0.68 to 11.75 ms (GPU-direct NV12), 4.78 to 23.38 ms (CPU-decoded MJPEG) and 3.28 to 13.20 ms (CPU-converted YUY2) ([../benchmarks/io-split.md](../benchmarks/io-split.md)).
- A source switch stops the old camera before it opens the new one. The new camera's first frame came 0.65 to 1.05 s after the request, and after 500 ms without input the output sent black placeholders: every switch showed about half a second of black.
- The output's `appsrc` queue was sized to the catch-up burst after a stall just under the 1 s resynchronisation threshold (31 buffers at 30 fps). Behind a sink slower than the contract rate, up to about a second of frames could wait in that queue, every one of them stale when it arrived.

## Decision

### Phase-locked emission
- **Timestamps are unchanged.** Tick `n` carries `D(n) = n x period`, every index is emitted exactly once and in order, and the caps never change.
- **Emission instant** of tick `n` = `D(n) + phase`, with the phase bounded to `[-period, 0]`: never after the timestamp, never more than one period before it. A phase move within that band never makes a tick late: the next emission instant is never before the current one. A move towards the early edge (a jump, or a wrap from 0 to `-period`) can make the next tick due at once, so two frames leave back to back, in order and neither after its timestamp. "Late" and "skipped" keep their meaning (a scheduling delay of the clock thread), now relative to the emission instants.
- **Steering.** Each fresh tick records how long its frame waited in the slot. The last `window` waits are kept, corrected for every phase change made since they were measured, so they predict the waits at the current phase without a loop delay. The smallest predicted wait belongs to the latest-arriving frame of the window; the phase is moved so that it equals the `margin`. Taking the minimum rather than the mean makes the margin a margin against the late side of the arrival jitter.
  - **Acquisition** (the first lock on an input run) moves the phase in one jump. A run ends at a new source, at a placeholder, at a hold, and when the same source's frames stop for more than 2.5 periods (they may come back at another phase).
  - **Tracking** moves it by at most `slew` per fresh tick, which follows camera clock drift (a 59.993 fps camera against a 30 fps contract drifts 0.004 ms per tick) without making the emission intervals jitter with the estimate.
  - **Re-acquisition**: when the smallest predicted wait exceeds the margin by more than `jump` (the arrivals moved earlier, for example after a camera hiccup), one jump again.
  - **Wrap**: a phase that has to leave its band wraps by a multiple of the input frame interval, the one landing nearest the middle of the band. For a camera at the contract rate this is one period: one emission interval is near zero (a camera slower than the contract shows one repeated frame there) or two periods long (a faster one supersedes one frame); the count of frames is unchanged. A 60 fps camera at a 30 fps contract moves to its other frame train, half a period away, without a repeat. Past the late edge the phase wraps at once, since waits shorter than the margin risk missed ticks; past the early edge it waits for `wrap hysteresis` of excess, so arrival jitter at an edge cannot wrap it back and forth.
- **Engagement.** The lock is engaged only while it can follow the input: the input runs at about the contract rate or a multiple of it, within the drift the slew can follow. Both conditions are measured over the frames of the same run:
  - a coarse check: the frame interval over the kept frames is at most 1.05 periods. It reacts within one window when a camera lowers its rate in dim light or runs a 15 fps mode;
  - a drift check: the ticks catch every `k`-th frame of the camera (`k x interval` nearest a period: 1 for a 30 fps camera, 2 for a 60 fps one), and that frame train moves against the ticks by `period - k x interval` per tick. The lock follows it only while that drift is at most `slew` per tick when the train moves earlier (a camera faster than `k` times the contract), and at most half the slew when it moves later (a slower camera). A slower camera's frames move towards the late edge of their slots, so a tracking loop that lags behind them under arrival jitter makes them miss their ticks; a faster camera's frames move away from it, and a lag only lengthens their wait. The interval is the slope of a least-squares fit of arrival time on sequence number over the run's last 64 fresh frames (about 2 s at 30 fps), so arrival jitter and a frame off its cadence barely move it. While the run has few frames the estimate is uncertain, and a drift within three standard errors (from the fit's residuals) of the slew counts as followable: the lock acquires after three frames and lets go once the drift is known to be out of reach, within about a second (a few seconds for a drift just beyond the limit). Once it has let go with a full window of 64 frames, it engages again only while the drift is within half the limit (plus the same allowance): an estimate near the limit would otherwise switch the lock on and off with its noise, and each time it engaged it would track from a held phase the frames had meanwhile moved away from.

  Not engaged, the phase is held where it is, and the output pads a slow input with repeats and thins a fast one exactly as a free-running clock at that phase would. A run is acquired once: when it engages again, tracking resumes from the held phase. On the CPU-decode reference camera about one frame in fifty is dequeued some 19 ms off its cadence, which stretches the coarse interval past its limit for a moment; re-acquiring each time cost 37 to 39 phase jumps a minute in measurement and simulation, and missed frames with them.

  Without the drift check, a camera at 29.5 to 29.75 or 30.25 to 31 fps against a 30 fps contract, or at 58 fps against 60, engaged the lock although the slew cannot follow it: the lock fell behind, frames missed their ticks and the phase jumped again and again (in simulation, a 29.5 fps camera with 0.5 ms of arrival jitter over 120 s: 337 phase jumps, 242 superseded frames, 302 repeats and a median wait of 31.4 ms, against no jump, 2, 62 and 16.6 ms for the free-running clock). With it, such a camera gives the free-running clock's figures at the held phase (test `phase_lock_lets_go_of_drift_beyond_its_slew`).

  With the slew as the limit on both sides, a camera at 29.8 to 29.9 fps against 30 fps with 1 to 2 ms of arrival jitter sat at the edge of the gate or inside it: the lock engaged and let go in turn, or tracked a drift of nearly the slew, and frames missed their ticks (in simulation, a 29.82 fps camera with 2 ms of jitter over 120 s: 55 phase jumps, 547 repeats and 526 superseded frames, against 86 and 65 for the free-running clock). With half the slew on the slow side and the hysteresis, the same camera gives one acquisition, 92 repeats and 70 superseded frames. Such a camera, from the moment the lock lets go, gives exactly the free-running clock's ticks at the held phase; before that, while the drift is not yet known, the lock tracks it and a few frames can miss (at most 7 more than the free-running clock in the test cases at 2 ms of jitter; test `phase_lock_lets_go_of_a_slow_drift_near_its_limit`). Cameras at 29.93 to 30.17 fps stay engaged, with a median wait of 2 to 5 ms against 15 to 17 ms free-running (1 to 2 ms of jitter).
- **Parameters** (`PhaseLockConfig`, defaults), chosen from a simulation sweep (cameras at 59.993, 60.01, 29.97, 30.03 and 30 fps, uniform arrival jitter of 0.25, 1 and 2 ms, and a skewed jitter: 1 ms plus one frame in ten up to 5 ms later; `phase_lock_tuning_sweep` in `crates/obc-pipeline/src/clock.rs`) and from hardware runs:

  | Parameter | Default | Why |
  |---|---|---|
  | margin | 1.5 ms | the smallest value swept (0.5 to 3 ms) that misses no frame in simulation up to 2 ms of uniform jitter. 1.0 ms already misses frames there (2 in 18000 at 1 ms of jitter, 82 at 2 ms) and, on the reference GPU-direct camera, 166 of 1799 in a 60 s run against 1 of 1800 at 1.5 ms; 2 ms adds 0.5 ms to every wait and misses no fewer frames up to 2 ms of jitter |
  | window | 8 fresh ticks | 4 or 6 miss frames at 2 ms of jitter (56 and 8); 12 or 16 add 0.15 to 0.3 ms to the median wait |
  | acquire after | 3 fresh ticks | two frame intervals are needed to tell whether the input is at the contract rate |
  | slew | 0.2 ms per fresh tick | 0.05 and 0.1 ms lag behind drift (median wait +0.05 to +0.1 ms); 0.4 ms misses frames at 2 ms of jitter and raises the emission-interval noise by a third or more |
  | jump | 8 ms | with the skewed jitter, 3 ms adds re-acquisitions (11 jumps against 5 over five simulated cameras), 2 ms 43, 1 ms 216, each risking a missed frame; 4 ms and more add none. 8 ms leaves room for a CPU decode whose time varies by several milliseconds; a phase move below it is slewed within about a second, and a new source acquires anew whatever this is |
  | wrap hysteresis | 1 ms, early edge only | none lets jitter wrap back and forth (20 wraps against 8 at 2 ms of jitter); 2 ms raises the 99th-percentile wait by 0.7 ms |
  | engagement | input interval at most 1.05 periods over the window, and drift against the ticks at most the slew (a faster camera) or half the slew (a slower one), fit over 64 frames with three standard errors of allowance; once let go with a full window, half that limit to engage again | the coarse check excludes 15 and 20 fps within a window; the drift check keeps the sweep's cameras (29.97 to 30.03 and 59.993 to 60.01 fps, jitter up to 2 ms) engaged and lets go of cameras the slew cannot follow; on the slow side, where a lag makes frames miss their ticks, the full slew left a band of cameras (29.8 to 29.9 fps) with several times the free-running clock's repeats |

### Held frame during a deliberate switch
- `Session` arms a **hold** on the output before it stops the current ingest, naming the new source (`Output::hold_for`), with a timeout (`OutputConfig::hold_timeout`, default 3 s; `obc-bench --hold-timeout`). Both `SIGUSR1` and `--switch-every` switch through the session.
- While the hold lasts, the output repeats the last frame whatever its age; the 500 ms gap threshold does not apply. Frames of the previous camera that still arrive are emitted but do not end the hold. The first fresh frame of the awaited source ends it, and so does the timeout, after which the gap threshold applies again: placeholders follow at once, the last frame being older than 500 ms by then. A hold never brings back a frame after placeholders were already being sent.
- The first start (no previous source), `Session::stop_ingest` and an ingest that fails arm no hold: an unexpected loss still turns black after 500 ms, so a dead camera never shows a frozen image for long. A switch whose new source fails to start holds for the timeout, then sends placeholders. So does a switch whose previous source fails to stop: the hold is armed before the stop, the new source is then dropped without being started, and the hold runs to its timeout.
- The request crosses threads through a mutex the clock thread takes once per tick; the deadline is an `Instant`, converted to the clock's own time origin there. The hold logic itself is in the pure `Pacer`.

### Bounded output queue
- `appsrc` holds at most `OutputConfig::queue_buffers` buffers (default 3) with `leaky-type` downstream: the oldest buffer is dropped when a new one arrives on a full queue, and counted (`queue_dropped`). The highest queue level seen after a push is reported (`queue_level_max`).
- The clock's catch-up burst (`PacerConfig::max_burst`) is the same bound: a wake-up with more ticks due than the queue holds resynchronises and counts the ticks skipped, instead of bursting stale frames into the queue. The 1 s resynchronisation threshold stays as an outer limit.
- Three buffers absorb the normal hand-off jitter (the queue level seen after a push was 1 in the runs, 2 with the roundtrip stage) and one late wake-up of up to two periods.

### Measurement
Since emission instants now follow the camera within one period, `obc-bench` judges the output rate (`fps_sustained`) by what the contract promises: the PTS sequence at the sink is exact (every tick once, in order), the frame count is within 0.1 % (at least one frame) of the rate, and the deviation of each frame's sink arrival from its PTS grid instant spans at most one period plus a 5 ms jitter allowance. A span of at most one period bounds every wall-clock 1 s window to the rate plus or minus one frame; the bench also reports the fewest and most frames seen in any 1 s window, and keeps the earlier grid-window figure for comparison ([../benchmarks/io-split.md](../benchmarks/io-split.md)).

## Alternatives considered

| Alternative | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Emit on arrival, the clock filling gaps only | Needs a wake-up from every ingest callback into the clock thread; emission instants inherit the raw arrival jitter; and a window that opens on the fixed grid to fill gaps re-locks emissions to that grid (for a 60 fps camera into 30 fps, a mean wait near half a period) unless its opening is phase-tracked, which is the phase lock anyway |
| Re-phase only at a switch | Does not follow camera clock drift: a 59.993 fps camera drifts about 70 ms against the grid in 10 minutes, so the wait sweeps the whole period |
| Follow the input timestamps (as `videorate` does) | The output rate would follow the camera's clock instead of the contract's exact rate |
| Fixed grid ([ADR-0009](0009-ingest-process-output.md)) | The wait for the next tick is up to a period, set by chance |
| A longer gap threshold instead of a hold | Would also freeze the image for that long when a camera dies, not only during a deliberate switch |
| `appsrc` sized to the 1 s catch-up burst | A slow sink receives up to a second of stale frames |

## Consequences
- **Added latency** is the ingest and output work plus the margin plus the camera's arrival jitter that the lock has to wait for. The camera's own frame timestamps on the reference cameras vary by 2.0 to 2.5 ms either way (1st to 99th percentile), so the median wait in the slot is about 3 ms, not the margin, and this jitter sets the floor of the median added latency (3.9 ms on the GPU-direct path, against 0.68 ms for the single pipeline and 11.75 ms for the fixed grid). Measured medians and the comparison are in [../benchmarks/io-split.md](../benchmarks/io-split.md).
- **Missed frames.** A frame that arrives after its tick misses it: a 60 fps camera's previous frame (16.7 ms older) is emitted instead, and a 30 fps camera's tick repeats the previous frame. The margin is the smallest value of the simulation sweep that missed no frame; it makes misses rare, not impossible, since a camera's jitter can have a longer tail than the simulated one. Measured on the reference cameras: 1 in 1800 frames on each 60 fps path in 60 s, at most 2 in 1800 on the CPU-decode path, and 4 in 18000 over ten minutes on the GPU-direct path (two of the camera's own dropped frames may be among them). A smaller margin trades a lower median for more misses (1.0 ms: 0.5 ms lower median, a tenth of the frames missed).
- **Emission intervals** stay at one period while the lock tracks, change by up to the slew per tick, and are irregular once at an acquisition, a re-acquisition or a wrap (between zero and two periods). The count over any span stays within one frame of the exact rate.
- The sink receives each frame up to one period before its PTS. `v4l2sink` runs with `sync=false`, and consumers of the loopback see the frames when they are written, so nothing waits for the PTS.
- **A switch shows the previous camera's last frame** until the new camera's first frame, instead of about half a second of black. A switch to a camera that never delivers, or one whose previous camera fails to stop, shows a frozen image for the hold timeout (3 s) before black.
- **A slow consumer** gets frames at most about three of its own service times old, and the drops are counted. A clock stall longer than three periods resynchronises (ticks counted as skipped) instead of bursting up to a second of frames.
